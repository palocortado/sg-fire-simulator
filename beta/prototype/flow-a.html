Why does this happen?
Prices rise in most years. If your money earns less than prices rise, each year it buys a little less, even though the number in your account goes up. We assume prices rise 2.5% a year, which is about the long-run Singapore average since the 1960s. Recent years have been lower. The point is the direction, not the exact figure.

What is an index fund, really?
Instead of choosing one company, you own a tiny slice of thousands of large companies. When the economy grows over the years, those companies grow too. Some years it falls, sometimes a lot, which is why it only suits money you won't need for 10 years or more. It isn't a bet against anyone: it grows when businesses grow. If a global fund feels too far away, a Singapore-only fund is a simpler first step, though less spread out.
