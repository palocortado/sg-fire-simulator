What is Financial Independence?

Financial Independence (FI) is the tipping point where your investments generate enough passive income to cover your living expenses indefinitely. You no longer have to work for money—giving you the ultimate freedom to design your time and life. It's the opportunity you've been waiting for - to tell your boss or your clients to go to hell and to know that you'll be just fine.

This simulator helps you visualize that finish line. By mapping out your current savings rate, investment choices, and desired future lifestyle, you can see exactly when you will cross the FI threshold—and discover which levers to pull to get there sooner.

Cash Savings

A realistic blend across high-yield account caps.

CPF
ℹ️
Before 55: your OA and SA are locked, shown as a dashed line. Your OA can still pay your mortgage.

At 55: your SA, then your OA, move into a Retirement Account (RA) up to your Full Retirement Sum (S$220,400 for people turning 55 in 2026; the engine grows this 3.5% a year for later years). Anything above that becomes available to you; if you are short of it, you can still take out S$5,000. Any OA savings you add after 55 stay locked until your Retirement Account reaches its target (they can still pay your mortgage). With a property pledge (Advanced), only the Basic Retirement Sum (half) stays in the RA.

From 65: your RA pays you a monthly CPF LIFE income for life instead of a lump sum. The engine uses the CPF Board's Standard Plan table (about S$1,780 a month from 65 for a Full Retirement Sum set aside in 2026). The RA then drops out of your wealth line, because it's income, not savings you can spend.

The engine uses the floor interest rates (OA 2.5%, SA/RA 4%) without extra interest. It doesn't model the optional 20% RA withdrawal at 65, which permanently lowers your payouts.

CPF contributions only apply to the first S$8,000 of monthly salary (2026). The ceiling rises in steps, not with inflation, so inflation-linked CPF inflows may slightly overstate future contributions for high earners.

If your Retirement Account has less than S$60,000 when payouts start, you are not automatically placed in CPF LIFE. The engine then pays it out monthly until about age 90 (the Retirement Sum Scheme).

If you're already 55 or older, enter your Retirement Account balance in the RA field that appears.

One-off Windfalls & Expenses

One-off amounts in today's dollars, adjusted for inflation to the year they happen.

Windfalls (e.g. an inheritance, a large bonus, selling a car) are saved the same way you already save, as a one-off top-up to your investments and cash.

Safe Withdrawal Rate

Your plan's withdrawal rate
ℹ️
Your first-year retirement spending divided by the savings you'd need on the day you stop working. The engine works this out by simulating your plan to age 100, including your mortgage, CPF at 55, CPF LIFE and any income streams.

The widely quoted "4% rule" comes from US research on 30-year retirements. Shorter retirements, or ones with CPF LIFE income, can support higher rates; longer ones usually need lower rates.

Use the override if you want a stricter rate as an extra safety margin. It raises the Finish Line on the chart, but your pass/fail result still comes from the full simulation.

How this works

What it does. The simulator projects your cash, investments and CPF year by year until age 100. Until your target financial freedom age, it adds your monthly savings and CPF contributions. After that, it pays your living expenses and mortgage from your cash and investments, plus any income streams and CPF LIFE payouts. It then checks whether your money lasts.

What it models. Investment returns and inflation, an HDB or bank mortgage, CPF Ordinary and Special Accounts, the Retirement Account at 55, CPF LIFE from 65 to 70 (or monthly payouts to about 90 for balances under S$60,000), one-off windfalls, expenses and property downgrades, and regular income such as rent. Expert mode adds random market returns (Monte Carlo) to show how often a plan lasts.

What it doesn't model. Taxes, MediSave, healthcare costs beyond what you include in your expenses, SRS, insurance, the value of your home, CPF extra interest, and future changes to CPF rules. Investment fees are only included if you lower your expected return to allow for them.

Where the figures come from. CPF rules and payouts from the CPF Board; inflation from the Singapore Department of Statistics; interest rates from the Monetary Authority of Singapore; market returns from index providers and fund factsheets. Each figure's source is in its ℹ️ note. Figures are reviewed once a year.
